How to Get Roofing Leads Without Buying Them

Tired of paying $50-$80 per shared roofing lead? Here's how to generate your own roofing leads through your website, Google, and reviews.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 25, 2026Updated November 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

Roofers get jobs without buying leads by building their own sources of work: storm-damage content on their website before storm season, before-and-after photos of every job, door hangers or yard signs around each finished job, a steady flow of Google reviews, and service pages and blog posts that keep showing up in search. It takes a few months to build, so you can keep buying leads in the meantime, but the calls you earn this way don't get shared with other roofers.

Buying roofing leads gets expensive, and a shared lead often goes to several other roofers who got the same notification. By the time you call, the homeowner may have already talked to someone else.

Roofers who get off the lead-buying treadmill do it by building their own sources of work. It takes a few months, but once it's running, you stop paying per request and nobody else gets that call.

Storm season is a website opportunity

After a big storm, every homeowner in the affected area Googles the same things: "roof damage inspection," "roof repair after storm," "how to file a roof insurance claim." If your website has content that ranks for those searches, you can get a wave of calls without paying for each one.

Write blog posts about storm damage before storm season hits. "What to Do After Your Roof Gets Hit by Hail," "How to File a Roof Insurance Claim in Florida," "Signs of Storm Damage You Can See From the Ground." Publish them in spring so they've had time to index by summer when hurricane season starts.

Say you publish four storm-related posts in April. If a storm hits in July, those posts are already sitting in search results when homeowners start looking, and all it took was a few hours of writing.

Before-and-after photos are your best marketing asset

Roofing is visual. A before-and-after photo of a beat-up shingle roof next to the new GAF Timberline install is more convincing than any paragraph of copy you could write. Post them on your website, your GBP, and your social media.

Take a photo before you start every job. Take one after you finish. Take a few during the work. This takes 90 seconds total and gives you content that sells for years.

A project gallery page on your website with 30 or 40 real before-and-after photos tells a homeowner everything they need to know about the quality of your work. No testimonial or marketing line is as persuasive as photographic proof.

Neighborhood saturation after every job

Neighborhood saturation means marketing to the houses around every job you finish, while your truck and your work are fresh in the neighbors' minds. Say you just finished a reroof on a house. Every neighbor on that street saw your truck parked there for two days. Some of them have roofs in the same condition.

Leave a door hanger on the 20 houses closest to the job. Something simple: "We just replaced your neighbor's roof at [address]. If yours needs an inspection, we're offering free estimates this week. Call or text [number]." Door hangers are cheap, so say they cost 15 cents each: if one of those 20 homeowners calls, that call cost you three dollars.

Some roofers skip the door hangers and go with yard signs. A sign in the yard of a completed job for two weeks costs nothing (ask the homeowner) and brings in calls from people who drive by every day.

Google reviews are your ranking weapon

The Map Pack is the box of three local businesses with a map that Google shows at the top of local searches. In roofing, a big gap in review count can be the difference between getting into the Map Pack and getting buried, because Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. BrightLocal found that 42% of searchers click on those map results.

The roofers who collect reviews consistently follow what we call the review routine, starting with an automated text that goes out the day after the job wraps up: "Hey [name], thanks for letting us take care of your roof. If you have a minute, a Google review would really help us out: [link]." Then one reminder goes out two days later, and every review gets a reply.

If you do 8 to 10 jobs a month and send that text after every one, the reviews add up month after month instead of depending on whether you remembered to ask.

The long game: SEO content that compounds

Blog posts and service pages don't bring traffic the week you publish them. They start showing up in search results after a few weeks, and they build over months. Each post, like one about "roof repair cost in [your city]," is another way for a homeowner in your area to find you, and 15 of those posts across different roofing topics and neighborhoods add up without you paying per click.

The roofers who win long-term are the ones who publish consistently. Two posts a month for a year gives you 24 indexed pages targeting different keywords. Your competitor with a three-page website can't touch that.

Stop renting leads, start owning them

Every dollar you send to Angi or HomeAdvisor is rent. You get a lead for today and nothing for tomorrow. Every dollar you put into your website, your GBP, and your content is equity. The leads compound over time, they cost less every month, and they're exclusively yours.

The transition usually takes a few months. During that time you can keep buying leads if you need to fill the calendar. But start building the organic pipeline now so that six months from now, you can turn off the spigot and stop paying for leads your competitors are also getting.
