How to Get Roofing Leads Without Buying Them

Tired of paying $50-$80 per shared roofing lead? Here's how to generate your own roofing leads through your website, Google, and reviews.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 25, 20266 min read

Roofing leads on Angi and HomeAdvisor run $50 to $80 each, and you're sharing them with two or three other roofers who got the same notification. By the time you call, the homeowner has already talked to someone else.

The roofers I work with who've gotten off the lead-buying treadmill all did the same thing: they built their own lead sources. It takes a few months, but once it's running, your cost per lead drops to single digits and nobody else gets that lead.

Storm season is a website opportunity

After a big storm, every homeowner in the affected area Googles the same things: "roof damage inspection," "roof repair after storm," "how to file a roof insurance claim." If your website has content that ranks for those searches, you get a flood of leads without spending anything.

Write blog posts about storm damage before storm season hits. "What to Do After Your Roof Gets Hit by Hail," "How to File a Roof Insurance Claim in Florida," "Signs of Storm Damage You Can See From the Ground." Publish them in spring so they've had time to index by summer when hurricane season starts.

One roofer in Broward County published four storm-related posts in April. When a hailstorm hit in July, those posts brought in 23 leads in a single week. His total investment in those posts was about six hours of writing.

Before-and-after photos are your best marketing asset

Roofing is visual. A before-and-after photo of a beat-up shingle roof next to the new GAF Timberline install is more convincing than any paragraph of copy you could write. Post them on your website, your GBP, and your social media.

Take a photo before you start every job. Take one after you finish. Take a few during the work. This takes 90 seconds total and gives you content that sells for years.

A project gallery page on your website with 30 or 40 real before-and-after photos tells a homeowner everything they need to know about the quality of your work. No testimonial or marketing line is as persuasive as photographic proof.

Neighborhood saturation after every job

You just finished a reroof on a house. Every neighbor on that street saw your truck parked there for two days. Some of them have roofs in the same condition.

Leave a door hanger on the 20 houses closest to the job. Something simple: "We just replaced your neighbor's roof at [address]. If yours needs an inspection, we're offering free estimates this week. Call or text [number]." Cost per door hanger is about 15 cents. If one of those 20 homeowners calls, your cost per lead was three bucks.

Some roofers skip the door hangers and go with yard signs. A sign in the yard of a completed job for two weeks costs nothing (ask the homeowner) and generates leads from people who drive by every day.

Google reviews are your ranking weapon

In roofing, the difference between 30 Google reviews and 130 Google reviews is the difference between page two and the Map Pack. And the Map Pack is where 60 percent of local search clicks go.

The roofers who collect reviews consistently have an automated text that goes out the day after the job wraps up: "Hey [name], thanks for letting us take care of your roof. If you have a minute, a Google review would really help us out: [link]." Reminder two days later. That's it.

Starting from 30 reviews, sending that text after every job, and doing 8 to 10 jobs a month, you'll hit 100 reviews in about five months. The jump in visibility is noticeable well before you get there.

The long game: SEO content that compounds

Blog posts and service pages don't bring traffic the week you publish them. They start showing up in search results after a few weeks, and they build over months. A post about "roof repair cost in [your city]" might bring in one or two leads a month. But 15 of those posts across different roofing topics and neighborhoods stack up to 20 to 30 leads per month, all organic, all free.

The roofers who win long-term are the ones who publish consistently. Two posts a month for a year gives you 24 indexed pages targeting different keywords. Your competitor with a three-page website can't touch that.

Stop renting leads, start owning them

Every dollar you send to Angi or HomeAdvisor is rent. You get a lead for today and nothing for tomorrow. Every dollar you put into your website, your GBP, and your content is equity. The leads compound over time, they cost less every month, and they're exclusively yours.

The transition takes three to six months. During that time you can keep buying leads if you need to fill the calendar. But start building the organic pipeline now so that six months from now, you can turn off the spigot and stop paying for leads your competitors are also getting.
