The Contractor's Guide to Getting Found on Google Without Paying for Ads

You don't need a big ad budget to rank on Google. Here's how contractors show up in local search results using free strategies that compound over time.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 15, 2026Updated November 24, 20267 min read
The short answer

Contractors get found on Google without ads by setting up and keeping up a Google Business Profile, building a website with a page for each service and town, asking every customer for a review, listing the business on directories that matter, and linking their own pages to each other. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and there's no way to pay for better local ranking, so these free steps are how you compete. It takes steady effort over months, and there's no guaranteed timeline.

Google Ads can cost thousands of dollars a month in competitive trades like roofing and HVAC. Some contractors can afford that. Many can't, especially in their first year or two.

The good news is that the organic results on Google, the listings below the ads, are free. So is the Map Pack. Getting into those spots takes time and effort instead of money, and once you're there, you have a good shot at staying there as long as you keep it up.

Here's what actually moves the needle.

Step 1: Google Business Profile first

This is the fastest path to local visibility and it costs nothing. A Google Business Profile (GBP) is the free listing that shows your business on Google Search and Google Maps. The Map Pack is the box of three local businesses with a map that Google shows for local searches, and your GBP is what gets you into it. BrightLocal found that 42% of searchers click on those map results.

What to do right now if you haven't already: claim your profile at business.google.com. Set your primary category to your specific trade (not "contractor" but "plumber" or "roofing contractor" or "HVAC contractor"). Add every service you offer as a separate service entry. Set your service area to the cities you actually serve. Upload at least 10 real photos of your work, your truck, and your team. Write a business description with your location and services mentioned naturally.

Then maintain it. Post an update every week or two. Add new photos after every job. Respond to every review. An active profile gives homeowners more reasons to trust you and gives Google more to work with.

Step 2: Build a website with real content

A one-page website with your phone number and a paragraph about your company isn't going to rank for anything useful. Google needs content to work with.

At minimum, build pages for your top five services, each targeting "[service] in [city]." Then add one or two location pages for your biggest markets. Then start a blog where you answer questions homeowners actually search for.

The blog is what scales. Each post is a new page that can rank for a new keyword. "How much does a roof replacement cost in [city]" or "what to do when your AC stops blowing cold air" bring in homeowners who are actively dealing with a problem you can solve.

You don't have to publish every day. Two posts a month adds up to 24 new ranking opportunities per year. After a year of that, your website gives homeowners far more ways to find you.

Step 3: Reviews are a ranking factor

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. A roofer with 180 reviews and a 4.9 rating has an edge over a roofer with 15 reviews and the same rating, all else being equal.

This is the one ranking factor you can influence every single day. We call this the review routine: a short text with your Google review link after every job, one friendly reminder a few days later, and a reply to every review that comes in. After every completed job, send a text with your Google review link. Keep it short and genuine: "Hey [name], thanks for letting us handle your [service]. If you've got a minute, a review would mean a lot: [link]."

Consistency beats volume. A steady flow of two or three new reviews a week looks far better to homeowners than 30 all at once and then nothing for three months.

Step 4: Get listed in directories that matter

Not all directories are worth your time. The ones that move the needle for contractors: Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor (business page), Thumbtack (even if you don't buy leads, the listing helps SEO), your local Chamber of Commerce, and any trade-specific directories for your industry.

A citation is any listing of your business name, address and phone number on another website, and each directory listing creates one. Google cross-references these citations to verify your business is real and located where you say you are. Consistency matters. Your business name and address should be identical across every listing.

Skip the low-quality directories that email you saying "claim your free listing." If you've never heard of the directory, your customers haven't either, and the link isn't worth much to Google.

Step 5: Internal linking between your pages

This is the technical SEO move most contractor sites miss entirely. When you write a blog post about roof repair, link to your roof repair service page. When someone reads your service page about AC installation, link to a blog post about "how to choose the right AC unit for your house."

Internal links are links from one page on your website to another page on your website. They help Google understand the structure of your site and which pages are most important, and they also keep visitors on your site longer.

A rule of thumb: every page on your site should link to at least two other pages. Every blog post should link to a relevant service page. This takes two minutes when you're publishing a new post and it compounds over time.

How long does this take to work?

There's no guaranteed timeline, but it usually takes months, not weeks, before you see meaningful organic traffic, because Google takes time to find and rank new content. Changes to your Google Business Profile can show up sooner.

Once it starts working, the calls that come from organic search don't cost you anything per click, and they keep coming as long as you maintain the site.

The contractors who start this today and stick with it will be in a much better spot a year from now. The ones who keep saying "I'll get to it next month" will keep paying for ads at next month's prices.
