Build Boosters vs. Hiring a Freelance Web Designer

A freelancer and a monthly plan can both get you a good contractor website. The right choice depends on how you want to pay, who you want making changes, and how much you want to handle yourself after launch.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

A freelance web designer is usually the better fit if you want a one-time project, you have a few thousand dollars ready up front, and you are comfortable handling hosting, updates and fixes yourself or paying for them as they come up. A monthly plan like Build Boosters is usually the better fit if you would rather pay a set amount each month with setup included, have someone else make changes for you, and get call, text and review tools on the same system as your site.

I run Build Boosters, so I obviously have a side in this comparison. I've tried to keep it fair anyway, because a freelancer really is the right answer for some contractors, and you should pick the option that fits how you run your business instead of the one that sounds best in a sales pitch.

What hiring a freelance web designer looks like

With a freelancer, you usually pay for a project. You agree on a scope, like a certain number of pages, a contact form and a gallery, and the designer quotes a price for building it. Many freelancers ask for part of the payment up front and the rest at launch. Once the site is live, the project is done, and anything after that, like new pages, edits or fixes, is usually billed by the hour or under a separate maintenance agreement.

A good freelancer can build you something custom that looks exactly how you want, and you get one person who knows your site well. The trade-off is that the quality and the follow-through depend completely on who you hire, so check their past work and ask how they handle changes after launch before you sign anything.

What a monthly website plan looks like

With a monthly plan, the build and the ongoing care are bundled into one price. Our Website plan is $79 a month and includes an SEO website with service and area pages, hosting, your domain name, quote forms, tap-to-call and unlimited updates done within 72 hours. Setup is included, there is no long contract, and paying yearly gets you two months free. The All-In-One plan at $197 a month adds tools like missed-call text-back, automated follow-up, an online booking calendar and automatic review requests. You can see every plan on the pricing page.

When a freelancer is the better choice

  • You want a fully custom design and you are willing to pay for it up front
  • You would rather own a finished project outright than pay every month
  • You or someone on your team is comfortable logging in to make your own edits
  • You already have the phone, texting and review tools you need, so all you want is the website
  • You have a freelancer you already know and trust who understands your trade

If that sounds like you, hiring a freelancer can be a great decision. Just make sure the agreement says who registers the domain, who pays for hosting, and what changes cost after launch.

When a monthly plan is the better choice

  • You would rather not spend thousands of dollars before the site is even live
  • You want someone else to handle edits, so you never have to log in to anything
  • You want service and area pages built in from the start, which helps Google understand what you do and where, as we explain in why your website needs more than one page
  • You miss calls during the day and want an automatic text reply and follow-up running on the same system as your site
  • You want to be live in days instead of waiting on a project schedule

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

Whichever way you go, these questions will save you trouble later, and any good freelancer or company should be happy to answer them.

  1. Who owns the domain and who is it registered to? It should be registered to your business, and you should be able to get it if you leave.
  2. What does it cost to make a change after launch? Ask for a real example, like adding a new service page.
  3. Who pays for hosting, and how much is it each year? This is easy to forget when you compare one-time quotes.
  4. Will the site have a page for each service and each town? One long page makes it harder for Google to match you to specific searches.
  5. How fast does it load on a phone? Think with Google found that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds to load.

The bottom line

There isn't one right answer here. If you want a custom site you pay for once and you are fine handling the upkeep, a good freelancer is worth it. If you want a set monthly price, someone else making the changes and your call and review tools in one place, a monthly plan will probably fit better.
