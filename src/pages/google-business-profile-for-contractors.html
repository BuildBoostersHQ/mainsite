Google Business Profile for Contractors: How to Show Up in the Map Pack

When a homeowner searches "roofer near me," the map results at the top get most of the calls. Here's how to give your business the best shot at being there.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 12, 2026Updated November 24, 20267 min read
The short answer

Google ranks local results based on three things it describes publicly: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher), and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are). To show up more, verify your profile, pick the right primary category, list your services and service areas, add real job photos, keep getting reviews, and link to a website with pages for each service and town.

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your business in Google Maps and in the map results at the top of a local search, with your reviews, photos, hours and a big call button. For a lot of contractors, it's the first thing a new customer sees. It's free, and it's one of the most valuable things you can spend an hour on.

How Google decides who shows up

The Map Pack is the box of three local businesses with a map that Google shows at the top of a local search. Google says it ranks local results using three main factors:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the searcher or the location in the search.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, including your reviews and what's said about you across the web.

You can't change where a homeowner lives. But you can do a lot about relevance and prominence.

Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your business on Google. If a profile already exists, claim it. If not, create one at Google Business Profile. Google will ask you to verify that you're the owner, which can mean a video, a phone call, a postcard or another method. Don't skip this. Unverified profiles don't show up well.

Step 2: Use your real business name

Enter your business name exactly as it appears on your truck, your sign and your paperwork. It's tempting to add keywords like "Ruiz Roofing Miami Best Roof Repair," but that goes against Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.

Step 3: Pick the right categories

Your primary category is one of the biggest relevance signals you control. Pick the one that best describes your main business, like "Roofing contractor" or "Plumber." Then add secondary categories for other work you do, like "Gutter cleaning service" or "Water heater installation service." Look at what top competitors in your area use for ideas.

Step 4: Set your service area

A service-area business is one that goes to its customers instead of having customers come to it, which describes most contractors. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their address should hide it and list the cities or areas they serve instead. Keep the list realistic. Add the towns you really work in, not the whole state.

Step 5: Fill out everything

  • Services: list each service you offer, with a short description
  • Hours: including special hours for holidays
  • Phone number: the one you actually answer, or one with text-back set up
  • Website: link to your homepage or the most relevant page
  • Description: a plain-English summary of what you do, where, and what makes you different
  • Attributes: like "veteran-owned" or "online estimates," if they apply

Step 6: Add real photos, often

Photos of your actual jobs, your crew and your trucks build trust fast. Before-and-afters are especially good. Skip the stock photos. Add new ones regularly, a few after every job, so your profile always looks active.

Step 7: Keep the reviews coming

Reviews are a big part of prominence, and they're what homeowners look at first when they compare contractors. We call this the review routine: ask every customer by text right after the job with a direct link, send one friendly reminder, and reply to every review. We wrote a whole guide on how to get more Google reviews if you want the step-by-step version.

Step 8: Post updates

Google lets you share posts on your profile, like a recent project, a seasonal offer or a quick tip. They don't take long, and they give people one more reason to pick you.

Step 9: Back it up with your website

Your profile and your website work together. A website with a page for each service and each area you serve gives Google more proof that you're relevant for those searches. Make sure your business name, phone number and address (if you show one) match everywhere: your website, your profile, and any directories you're listed in.

Common mistakes to avoid: keyword-stuffing your business name, using a fake address or virtual office, creating duplicate profiles for the same business, and letting your hours or phone number go out of date.

The bottom line

Your Google Business Profile is free advertising that shows up exactly when people are looking for you. Fill it out completely, keep it accurate, add photos and reviews every month, and connect it to a website that backs it up. It won't happen overnight, but it compounds.

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing your Google Business Profile is free.

Should I show my address on Google if I work from home?

If customers don't come to your location, Google's guidelines say to hide your address and list your service areas instead.

How long does it take to show up in the Google map results?

It varies by trade and area. A newly verified profile can start showing up within weeks, but climbing higher usually takes months of steady reviews, photos and activity.

Can I put keywords in my business name on Google?

No. Your business name on Google should match your real-world name. Adding extra keywords goes against Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.
