What Missed Calls Really Cost Contractors (and How to Stop Losing Them)

You can't answer the phone from a ladder. But every call you miss is a homeowner who might be dialing the next contractor on the list.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 12, 20265 min read
The short answer

A missed call often turns into a lost job, because many homeowners call the next contractor instead of leaving a voicemail. To see what it costs you, multiply missed calls per week by 52, by your close rate, and by your average job. The fix is to respond instantly, even when you can't pick up: an automatic text back the moment a call is missed, so the customer knows you got it and stays with you.

Think about the last time you needed a plumber. You probably searched, picked one with good reviews, and called. If nobody answered, did you leave a voicemail and wait? Or did you call the next one? Your customers do the same thing. That's why missed calls are one of the most expensive problems in a contracting business, and one of the easiest to fix.

Why missed calls happen

It's not because you don't care. It's because you're working. You're on a roof, under a sink, running a saw, or talking to another customer. Small crews can't have someone sitting by the phone all day, and calls don't wait for your lunch break.

Do the math for your business

Here's a simple way to see what missed calls are costing you:

Missed calls per week × 52 weeks × your close rate × your average job = what you're leaving on the table each year.

Say you miss 3 calls a week, you usually win 1 out of 5 jobs you quote (20%), and your average job is $1,500. That's 3 × 52 × 0.20 × $1,500 = $46,800 a year. And that's before counting the repeat work and referrals those customers would have brought.

Your numbers will be different. Plug them into the lost-jobs calculator on our homepage to see yours.

Why voicemail isn't enough

Lots of callers hang up instead of leaving a message, especially when the job is urgent. And even when they do leave one, by the time you call back that evening, they may have already booked someone else. Speed wins. The contractor who responds first usually gets the job.

The fix: missed-call text-back

Missed-call text-back is exactly what it sounds like. When you miss a call, the customer automatically gets a text from your business number a few seconds later. Something like:

Hey, this is Ruiz Roofing, sorry we missed your call! We're on a job right now. What can we help you with? We'll get back to you shortly.

That text does three things:

  • It tells the customer you got their call and you're real
  • It gives them an easy way to explain the job without waiting on hold
  • It keeps them talking to you instead of calling the next contractor

Most people will text back with what they need. You can reply between jobs, send a booking link, or call them back when you're free, and the job is still yours.

Set it up the right way

  1. Use a business number that can text. Your missed-call texts and replies all come from one number your customers recognize.
  2. Register it for business texting. In the US, business text messages go through a registration process (called A2P 10DLC) so your texts actually get delivered. A good provider handles this with you.
  3. Write a message that sounds like you. Short, friendly, and with your business name.
  4. Keep every conversation in one inbox. Calls, texts, form fills and chat in one place, so nothing slips.
  5. Add an after-hours version. Let night and weekend callers know when you'll get back to them.

Other ways to catch more calls

  • Put a quote form on your website for people who'd rather type than talk.
  • Add online booking for estimates, so customers can grab a time without a phone call.
  • Set a short, clear voicemail greeting that says you'll text them back soon.
  • Follow up on quotes that didn't get an answer. A friendly text a few days later wins back a surprising amount of work.

The bottom line

You're never going to answer every call, and you shouldn't have to. You just need every caller to hear from you fast. An automatic text-back is one of the cheapest, simplest ways to stop handing jobs to your competition.

Common questions

Have a question that isn't here? Call or text us at (786) 479-8856 and we'll get back to you the same day.

What is missed-call text-back?

It's an automation that sends a text from your business number to anyone whose call you miss, a few seconds after the call ends. It lets the customer know you got their call and gives them an easy way to keep the conversation going.

Will missed-call text-back work with my current phone number?

Often yes, depending on your phone setup. Many contractors either move their number to a system that supports texting or forward calls through a new business number.

Is it legal to text people who called me?

Replying to someone who just called your business is generally fine, but business texting in the US needs to be registered (A2P 10DLC) and you should always honor opt-out requests like STOP.

How fast should I respond to a new lead?

As fast as possible. The first contractor to respond usually has the best shot at the job, which is why an instant automatic text is so useful.
