Angi, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack Alternatives: How Contractors Get Their Own Leads

Lead sites can fill a slow week. But when every lead is shared and every click costs you, it pays to build something you own.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 12, 20266 min read
The short answer

Lead platforms like Angi, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack can bring you work, but you typically pay for each lead or contact, and the same customer is often talking to other pros too. The long-term alternative is to own your lead flow: a strong Google Business Profile, a steady stream of reviews, a website built to turn visitors into calls, fast follow-up on every lead, and staying in touch with past customers. Most contractors keep the platforms while they build this, then cut back.

Plenty of contractors started out on lead sites, and that's fine. When you're new and the calendar is empty, paying for leads can get you working. But over time, a lot of contractors start to feel like they're renting their business instead of owning it. Here's why, and what the alternative looks like.

How lead platforms usually work

Each platform has its own pricing, and it changes over time, so check their current terms. But the basic model is similar: you pay for leads or customer contacts, and the homeowner is often comparing you with other pros who got the same request. That means:

  • You pay whether or not you win the job
  • You're competing on speed and price with every other pro on the lead
  • The customer relationship starts on someone else's platform
  • When you stop paying, the leads stop

None of that makes lead platforms bad. It just makes them expensive as your only source of work.

What "owning your leads" means

Owning your leads means homeowners find you directly, call your number, and become your customer, with no middleman. It's built from a few pieces that work together.

1. Your Google Business Profile

This is the listing that shows up in Google Maps and the map results when someone searches for your trade in your area. It's free, and for many local contractors it's the biggest source of calls. Here's our guide to setting it up the right way.

2. A steady stream of reviews

Reviews help you show up and help you get picked. The contractors who dominate their area almost always have more recent reviews than everyone else. Ask every customer, every time. Here's how to get more Google reviews without being awkward.

3. A website built to convert

Your website is where people decide whether to call you. It needs a clear list of services, the areas you serve, real job photos, your reviews, and an easy way to call or request a quote from a phone. A page for each service and town also helps you show up in more searches.

4. Fast follow-up on every lead

Owning your leads doesn't help if they slip through the cracks. Respond to every call, text and form fast, even when you're on a job. An automatic missed-call text-back handles the calls you can't answer.

5. Your past customers

The cheapest lead is someone who already hired you. Stay in touch with past customers for repeat work, seasonal maintenance and referrals. A simple text a year later ("Time for your gutter cleaning?") brings back work you'd otherwise lose.

How to make the switch without a slow month

  1. Don't quit lead sites cold turkey. Keep them running while you build your own channels.
  2. Track where every job comes from. Just ask "How did you hear about us?" and write it down.
  3. Know your cost per job, not cost per lead. What you spent on a source divided by the jobs it actually won.
  4. Shift your budget as your own leads grow. When Google and referrals are filling the calendar, cut back on the paid leads that cost the most per job.
The goal isn't zero paid leads. It's a business where paid leads are optional, because your phone rings on its own.

The bottom line

Lead platforms are a useful tool, but a risky foundation. Every review you earn, every page on your website and every happy customer you stay in touch with keeps working for you long after you've paid for it. Start building that now, and the shared leads become a choice instead of a necessity.
