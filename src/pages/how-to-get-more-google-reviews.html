How to Get More Google Reviews as a Contractor: 5 Simple Steps

Most happy customers will leave a review. They just need you to ask at the right time, in the right way, with a link that takes one tap.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 12, 2026Updated November 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

Ask every customer, right after the job is done, by text, with a direct link to your Google review page. Keep the message short and personal, send one friendly reminder if they don't respond, and reply to every review you get. Don't offer anything in exchange for reviews and don't only ask the customers you think are happy, because both go against Google's rules.

You did great work. The customer said so. Then they never left a review. Sound familiar? It's not that they didn't mean it. Life got busy, and leaving a review wasn't on their to-do list. The fix is a simple, repeatable way of asking that makes it easy to say yes.

Why Google reviews matter so much

Reviews do two jobs at once. First, they help you show up: Google says review count and rating are part of how it decides which local businesses to show. Second, they help you get chosen. When a homeowner is comparing three contractors in the map results, the one with more recent, detailed reviews usually gets the call.

Step 1: Get your review link

In your Google Business Profile, look for the option to ask for reviews. Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly. Save it somewhere you can grab it fast, like a text shortcut on your phone. This one link is the whole game. If customers have to search for you and scroll to find the review button, most won't.

Step 2: Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is right after the job is done, while the customer is looking at the finished work and feeling good about it. Same day is ideal. A week later, the moment has passed.

A quick in-person mention helps too: "We'd really appreciate a Google review. I'll text you the link." That way the text doesn't come out of nowhere.

Step 3: Send a short, personal text

Text beats email because people actually read it. Keep it short, use their name, and put the link right in the message. Here's a template you can steal:

Hi Carla, thanks again for choosing Ruiz Roofing today! If you have 30 seconds, a quick Google review would help us a ton: [your link]

If they don't respond in a few days, send one friendly reminder. Then leave it alone.

Hey Carla, just a quick reminder in case it got buried. Here's the review link if you have a sec: [your link]. Thanks either way!

Step 4: Make it automatic

The hardest part of asking for reviews is remembering to do it after every single job. The contractors with hundreds of reviews aren't better at asking. They just never skip it. Set up a system that sends the review request automatically when a job is marked done, plus a reminder if they don't leave one. Then it happens every time, even on your busiest weeks.

Put together, the whole routine takes about a minute per job:

WhenWhat to send

What Google doesn't allow

Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile flagged, so it's worth knowing them:

  • No incentives. Don't offer discounts, gift cards or anything else in exchange for a review.
  • No "review gating." Don't screen customers first and only send the review link to the happy ones. Ask everyone.
  • No fake reviews. Not from friends, family, employees or paid services.
  • No review stations. Don't set up a tablet or kiosk at your office for customers to leave reviews on the spot.

Step 5: Reply to every review

Replying shows future customers that you care, and it shows Google that your profile is active. For good reviews, keep it short and specific: thank them by name and mention the job. "Thanks, Mike! Glad we could get that leak fixed before the storm."

How to handle a bad review

  1. Don't reply angry. Wait until you've cooled off.
  2. Keep it short and calm. Thank them, own anything that went wrong, and offer to make it right offline.
  3. Take it off the page. "Please call me directly at [number] so we can sort this out."
  4. Flag it only if it breaks Google's rules, like a review from someone who was never your customer.

A calm, professional reply to a bad review often earns more trust than a perfect 5.0 rating, because it shows how you treat people when something goes wrong.

The bottom line

Getting more reviews isn't about clever tricks. Ask every customer, at the right time, with one tap to the review page, and never skip it. Do that for a few months and you'll stand out from almost every other contractor in your area.

Common questions

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google doesn't allow incentives of any kind in exchange for reviews. Just ask, and make it easy.

How many Google reviews does a contractor need?

There's no magic number, but you want more recent reviews than the contractors you compete with in your area. A steady flow of new reviews matters more than a big batch from years ago.

Should I reply to every Google review?

Yes. A short, personal reply to every review shows future customers that you care and keeps your profile active.

Can I delete a bad Google review?

You can't delete reviews yourself. You can report one that breaks Google's rules, like spam or a review from someone who was never a customer. For honest negative reviews, reply calmly and offer to make it right.
