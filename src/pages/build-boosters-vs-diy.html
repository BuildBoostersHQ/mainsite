Build Boosters vs. DIY Website Builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy)

A website builder can save you money if you have the time. Here is an honest look at when DIY makes sense and when a done-for-you plan is the better deal.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

A DIY website builder like Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy is usually the cheapest way to get a contractor website online, and it is a good choice if you enjoy that kind of work and have evenings to spend on it. A done-for-you plan like Build Boosters costs more per month, but we build the site, write the service and area pages, make every change for you and can connect it to call, text and review tools, so you spend your time on jobs instead of on your website.

I run Build Boosters, so keep that in mind as you read. I still think website builders are good products, and for some contractors they are the smarter pick. The real question isn't which option is better in general, it's which one fits the time, budget and patience you actually have.

What DIY website builders do well

Website builders have come a long way. You pick a template, drag in your text and photos, connect a domain and publish, all without writing any code. They handle hosting and security for you, they usually include a simple contact form, and you can log in and change something at ten at night without waiting on anyone.

The price is the biggest draw. When I checked in November 2026, Squarespace's pricing page listed four website plans ranging from $19 to $99 a month when paid yearly, or $25 to $139 a month when paid month to month, with a free custom domain for the first year on annual plans. Wix and GoDaddy also sell several plan levels, and their prices change often and can vary by region, so check the Wix pricing page and the GoDaddy plans page for what you would pay today.

Where DIY gets harder for contractors

The hard part of a contractor website isn't the builder, it's everything you have to put into it. You need to write a page for each main service and each town you work in, pick good photos, set up a quote form that sends to the right place, make sure tap-to-call works on a phone and keep it all up to date as your services change. That is a lot of work to fit in around a full schedule of jobs, and it is the part that usually gets skipped.

Speed and phone use matter more than people expect, too. Every extra app, big photo and fancy effect you add can slow a page down, and a slow site on a phone costs you visitors.

When a DIY website builder is the better choice

  • You are just starting out and need the lowest possible monthly cost
  • You like design work and actually enjoy building and tweaking your own site
  • You have time each month to write new pages and keep things current
  • You only need a simple online presence for now and get most of your work from referrals

If that sounds like you, a builder is a perfectly good place to start. Use our contractor website checklist as you build so you don't miss the basics, like a clear phone number at the top, a page for each service and real photos of your work.

When a done-for-you plan is the better choice

  • You started a DIY site and it has been sitting half finished for months
  • You want separate service and area pages without having to write them yourself, which is a big part of why one-page sites struggle on Google
  • You want someone else to handle edits so you can stay on the job
  • You miss calls during the day and want an automatic text reply sent to every caller you can't pick up, which we explain in what missed calls cost contractors
  • You want review requests, booking and your messages in one place instead of stitching together separate apps

The hidden cost of DIY is your time

When you compare prices, it's easy to look only at the monthly plan and forget about the hours. Building a good contractor site from a template can take many evenings, and keeping it current takes more time every time you add a service, change your hours or start working in a new town. Put an honest value on your time and compare that, plus the builder's plan and any add-on apps, against a monthly plan that includes the work. Our breakdown of what a contractor website costs walks through that math in more detail.

It also helps to think about what your website needs to do once it is live. A site that looks nice but doesn't make it easy to call, text or request a quote isn't doing its job, which we cover in why contractor websites don't bring in work.

The bottom line

DIY website builders are a good, low-cost option if you have the time and enjoy the work, and there is no shame in starting there. If you would rather pay a set monthly price to have the site built, the pages written and the changes handled for you, a done-for-you plan will probably save you more time than it costs. Is Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy good for a contractor website?

All three can work for a contractor website if you put in the time to build service and area pages, use real photos and keep it fast on a phone. The builder matters less than what you put into it.

How much does a DIY contractor website cost?

A typical small-business plan on a website builder runs roughly $20 to $50 a month, and prices vary by plan, billing term and region. Add-on apps for things like booking or reviews can cost extra.

Can I switch from a DIY builder to Build Boosters later?

You can, and you can bring your logo, photos and service details with you. If your domain is registered with the builder, it can usually be pointed to or transferred to a new site.

Why is a done-for-you plan more expensive than a website builder?

With a builder you are paying for software and doing the work yourself. With a done-for-you plan you are paying for the build, the writing and the ongoing changes, and on All-In-One, the call, text and review tools as well.
