Contractor Website Checklist: 20 Things Your Site Needs

Go through this list with your own website open on your phone, tick off what you already have, and you'll know exactly what's missing before you spend another dollar on marketing.

The short answer

A contractor website has five jobs: help you get found on Google, earn a homeowner's trust, make it easy to call or ask for a quote, follow up fast when you can't pick up, and show you what's working. The 20 items below cover all five, and you can tick them off on this page or print the PDF version and go through it with a pen.

You don't need a fancy website to win jobs, but you do need one that covers the basics, because homeowners compare a few contractors quickly and pick the one that looks the most trustworthy and the easiest to reach. Each item below has a one-line reason, so you can decide what to fix first.

What is the most important thing on a contractor website?

The easiest wins are a phone number customers can tap on mobile and a short quote form on every page, because those turn a visit into a call. After that, real photos and reviews do the most to get you picked over the next contractor.

How many pages should a contractor website have?

There's no set number, but you want a home page, a page for each service you offer, a page for each city you work in, plus about, contact and reviews pages. A roofer with five services and six towns ends up with well over a dozen pages, and that's normal.

Can I check my own website speed?

You can run your site through Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool, which scores it on mobile and desktop and lists what's slowing it down. Large photos that haven't been compressed are one of the usual reasons a contractor site loads slowly.
