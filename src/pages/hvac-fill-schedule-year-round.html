How HVAC Companies Can Fill Their Schedule Year-Round

HVAC companies don't have to live and die by summer and winter. Here's how to keep your schedule booked in the shoulder months too.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 1, 2026Updated November 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

HVAC companies fill the slow spring and fall months by selling maintenance agreements on every call, publishing website content for the searches homeowners make in those seasons, texting past customers twice a year to book tune-ups, and responding to every new request within minutes. None of that needs a bigger ad budget. It works because the homeowners who need you in April and October are out there, they just aren't searching for emergency repairs yet.

A lot of HVAC companies deal with the same pattern, where summer and winter are slammed and spring and fall are dead. You're either turning down work or staring at an empty calendar.

The fix isn't spending more on ads during the slow months. It's building systems that keep leads trickling in all year and making sure the ones you already have don't go cold.

Why the shoulder months kill HVAC revenue

The obvious answer is that people call when their AC dies in July or their furnace quits in January. Emergency calls drive the busy season. When nothing is broken, nobody's thinking about HVAC.

But here's what most HVAC companies miss: the homeowners who need you in April and October absolutely exist. They want maintenance. They want an efficiency check before summer hits. They want to replace a 15-year-old unit before it fails during a freeze. They're just not searching "HVAC repair near me" because nothing is broken yet.

You have to reach them with a different message.

Maintenance agreements are your off-season engine

A maintenance agreement is a yearly plan where the homeowner pays a set price for regular tune-ups, usually twice a year, plus perks like priority scheduling. If you're not selling maintenance plans, you're leaving the easiest recurring revenue on the table. A biannual tune-up plan at $150 to $200 a year does two things: it fills your April and October calendar, and it puts you first in line when that customer does need a repair or replacement.

The companies that do this well make the pitch at the end of every install and every repair call. "For $179 a year, we come out twice, clean everything, check refrigerant, priority scheduling if something breaks." The pitch lands best when the tech makes it in person, right after the customer has seen the work.

Over two or three years, those maintenance customers stack up. Say you build up to 340 active maintenance agreements. That's 680 scheduled visits a year filling the gaps in your calendar before you even pick up the phone for a new customer.

Content that ranks during the slow months

Homeowners Google different things in spring than they do in summer. In March and April, they search "how to get AC ready for summer," "HVAC maintenance checklist," "how often should I change my air filter." In September and October it's "furnace tune-up," "how to lower heating bill," "when to replace furnace."

If your website has blog posts answering those questions, you show up when those homeowners are thinking about HVAC but haven't picked up the phone yet. That's a lead who's planning, not panicking. They're comparison shopping. They have time to look at your reviews, check out your website, and fill out a form.

These leads close at a lower rate than emergency calls because there's no urgency. But they also don't cost you anything per click, and they come in during the exact months you need them.

Reactivation campaigns for past customers

Past-customer reactivation means reaching back out, by text or email, to people you've already worked for so they book with you again. You've already done work for hundreds of homeowners, and a lot of them haven't heard from you since. A simple text or email campaign twice a year turns past customers into repeat customers.

Something like: "Hey [name], it's been about 6 months since we serviced your system at [address]. Want to get on the schedule for a tune-up before summer? Reply YES and we'll get you booked."

Say you send that text to 400 past customers in March and even a small share of them book. Those are jobs in a slow month from people who already trust you, with no ad spend at all.

Stop relying on emergency calls

The HVAC companies that stay busy year-round aren't doing anything exotic. They sell maintenance agreements at every job. They have website content that ranks for seasonal searches. They text past customers twice a year. They also respond to every new request within minutes instead of hours, which is what people call speed-to-lead.

None of that requires a bigger ad budget. It requires a system that runs whether you're on a roof or sitting at your kitchen table doing invoices at 9 PM.
