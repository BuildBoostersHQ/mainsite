Texting vs. Calling: Which Gets Contractors More Booked Jobs?

Should contractors text or call their leads? Texting usually wins the first contact, and calling closes the job. Here's when to use each.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 7, 2026Updated November 24, 20265 min read
The short answer

For the first contact, texting usually works better, because a customer can read and answer a text at work, while many people won't pick up a call from a number they don't recognize. Calling is better for closing, because you can answer questions and book the estimate in one conversation. The best approach uses both in order: an automatic text within a minute, a personal call 10 to 15 minutes later, and another text if they don't answer.

Say 40 people fill out your website form in a month and you call every one of them. A lot of them are at work or don't recognize your number, so plenty of those calls go unanswered and the job goes to whoever reaches them first.

Now say you send an automatic text within 60 seconds of every form and follow up with a call 10 minutes later. The customers who couldn't take a call can still reply to the text, so more of them actually hear from you while they're still interested, with the same ads and the same website.

Why texts work better for the first touch

The reason isn't complicated. Plenty of people ignore calls from numbers they don't recognize because of spam calls, but a text waits on their screen until they have a moment to read it.

When a homeowner fills out your quote form at 2 PM on a Tuesday, they're probably at work. They can't take a phone call from a number they don't recognize. But they'll read a text in the next 30 seconds and reply "yes, 5pm works" without leaving their desk.

The text also gives your lead a record of your name, your company, and that you responded. When you do call later, they see your number and think "oh right, the roofer who texted me." They pick up.

When to call vs. when to text

Texting is better for the first contact because speed-to-lead, which is how quickly you respond after someone reaches out, matters a lot, and plenty of calls go unanswered. But calling is better for closing the deal because you can answer questions, build rapport, and schedule the estimate in one conversation.

We call this the text-first sequence: an automated text within 60 seconds, personal call within 10 to 15 minutes, second text that evening if they haven't responded, one more call the next morning. After that, they go into a nurture sequence with a text every few days for two weeks. For a full ten-day version, see the six-text follow-up plan.

A lot of contractors make one call, leave a voicemail, and never follow up again, which means a request you may have paid for got exactly one shot.

Here is the order that tends to work best:

What to say in the first text

Keep it short and conversational. No one reads a paragraph from a business they just found on Google.

Good: "Hey, this is Mike with Tampa Bay Roofing. Got your quote request. When's a good time for a quick call?"

Bad: "Thank you for your inquiry regarding our roofing services. A member of our team will be reaching out to you shortly to discuss your project needs and provide a comprehensive estimate."

The second one sounds like it was generated by a computer because it was. The first one sounds like a human being who actually wants to help.

The voicemail problem

Here's something that frustrates me about how a lot of contractors handle new requests. They call, nobody answers, they leave a voicemail that says "Hi, this is Dave from Dave's Plumbing, give me a call back at 555-1234." Then they wait.

Plenty of people never call back after a voicemail, so relying on one isn't a strategy, it's a lottery ticket.

If you're going to leave a voicemail, follow it immediately with a text: "Hey, just left you a voicemail. Easier to text if that works better for you." That text will get seen. The voicemail probably won't even get listened to.

Stop choosing one or the other

The contractors booking the most jobs aren't picking between texting and calling. They're doing both in a specific order: text first for speed, call for the conversation, text again for the follow-up. The whole thing can be automated for the first touch so you don't have to be staring at your phone all day.

Your leads are already warm when they fill out your form. They searched for you, they looked at your site, they took the time to type in their info. The only question is whether you get back to them before the other three contractors they also contacted.
