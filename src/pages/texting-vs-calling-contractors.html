How to Get More Plumbing Leads (Without Cold Calling)

Cold calling homeowners is a waste of time for plumbers. Here are 6 ways to get plumbing leads that actually turn into booked jobs.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 10, 20266 min read

No homeowner wants a cold call from a plumber. And no plumber wants to make one. If you're sitting at your kitchen table at 7 PM dialing through a list from Whitepages, there are better ways to spend that time.

The plumbers I've worked with who consistently book 15 to 25 jobs a week aren't doing anything complicated. They've built a few systems that bring leads to them, and they respond fast when those leads come in.

Google Business Profile is your best free tool

If your GBP isn't optimized, stop reading and go do that first. When someone searches "plumber near me," Google shows three businesses in the Map Pack before any organic results. Getting into those three spots is worth more than any ad you'll ever run.

What "optimized" actually means: every service you offer listed individually (not "plumbing services" but "drain cleaning," "water heater installation," "sewer line repair," each as its own entry). Real photos of your work, your truck, your crew. Your service area set correctly. Posts published at least every couple weeks. And reviews. Lots of reviews.

A plumber in Fort Lauderdale I worked with went from 12 Google reviews to 85 in four months just by texting every customer a review link the day after the job. His Map Pack visibility went from nowhere to top three and he started getting 8 to 10 calls a week just from his GBP listing.

Your website needs to rank for specific services

"Plumber in [city]" is competitive. You probably won't rank for it right away. But "tankless water heater installation [city]" or "sewer camera inspection [city]" are way less competitive and those searchers are ready to buy.

Build a page for each service you offer. Each page needs at least 400 to 500 words, a clear heading with the service and location, a description of what you do and why a homeowner would need it, a ballpark on pricing if you're comfortable sharing, and a call-to-action to get a quote.

Over six months, those pages start ranking. I've seen a single service page bring in 3 to 5 leads per month on its own. Multiply that by 8 or 10 service pages and your website is generating 30 to 50 leads a month without ads.

Referrals from other trades

Plumbers work alongside electricians, HVAC techs, general contractors, and home inspectors. Those people get asked "do you know a good plumber?" constantly. If you're not building relationships with other tradespeople in your market, you're missing easy, high-quality referrals.

This doesn't require anything formal. Buy lunch for a few GCs and home inspectors. Leave a stack of cards with the electrician you always see on job sites. When someone sends you a referral, send them one back. Over time, 20 to 30 percent of your leads can come from referrals, and those leads close at two to three times the rate of cold internet leads because trust is already built.

Online reviews are your 24/7 sales team

A homeowner comparing two plumbers will pick the one with 150 five-star reviews over the one with 9 reviews, even if the second plumber is better at the actual work. That's how people make decisions now.

The key is making it easy. After every job, send an automated text with your Google review link. Something like: "Thanks for choosing us. If you've got 30 seconds, a review would help us out a lot: [link]." Send a reminder 48 hours later if they haven't clicked.

Don't overthink the wording. Don't offer incentives for reviews (Google penalizes that). Just ask consistently and the numbers stack up.

Nextdoor and Facebook groups

This is free and weirdly underused by plumbers. Join every neighborhood Facebook group and Nextdoor area in your service zone. When someone posts "anyone know a good plumber?" you want your name in that thread.

You don't need to spam or self-promote. Just answer questions when they come up, mention what you do when it's relevant, and be helpful. Homeowners check the profiles of people who recommend businesses. If your profile links back to your website, that's a lead.

One plumber I know gets 4 to 6 leads a month from Nextdoor alone. He spends maybe 10 minutes a day scanning for relevant posts. It's the highest ROI 10 minutes in his day.

Stop paying for shared leads

Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Thumbtack sell the same lead to three or four plumbers at once. You're paying $30 to $60 for a lead who's already getting called by your competitors. Close rates on shared leads are 10 to 15 percent on a good day.

Compare that to a lead who found your website on Google, read your reviews, looked at your photos, and filled out your quote form. That person already picked you. They're just waiting for you to confirm. Close rate on those leads runs 30 to 50 percent.

The investment to get there is a good website, an optimized GBP, and consistent content. It takes a few months to build up. But once it's running, your cost per lead drops to near zero and you own those leads completely.
