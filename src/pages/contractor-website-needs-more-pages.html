Why Your Contractor Website Needs More Than One Page

A one-page contractor website can't rank on Google. Here's why you need dedicated service and location pages to get found.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 28, 2026Updated November 24, 20265 min read
The short answer

A one-page contractor website doesn't rank well because Google ranks individual pages, and a single page can only match a handful of searches. A site with its own page for each service and each town can show up for each of those searches, because a page about one job in one city matches that search far better than a line in a list. A good starting point is a page for each of your top five services, two or three town pages, and a new blog post every couple of weeks.

A lot of contractors have a one-page website. Everything crammed onto the homepage: services, about, reviews, contact form. It looks clean. It was cheap to build. It's also almost invisible on Google.

Here's why.

Google ranks pages, not websites

When someone searches "water heater installation Fort Lauderdale," Google is looking for a page specifically about water heater installation in Fort Lauderdale. Your homepage that mentions water heaters in a bullet list alongside 11 other services doesn't match that search the way a dedicated page does.

A competitor with a page titled "Water Heater Installation in Fort Lauderdale" that spends 500 words talking about their process, pricing, and the brands they install will usually outrank your one-page site, even if your work is better and even if you've been in business longer. Google rewards relevance, and a dedicated page is more relevant than a mention in a list.

The math: 1 page vs. 10 pages

A one-page site has one chance to rank. It's targeting whatever your homepage title says, probably something generic like "ABC Plumbing - Quality Service in South Florida."

Now imagine 10 pages: your homepage, a page each for drain cleaning, water heater installation, pipe repair, sewer line work, and bathroom remodeling, a page each for Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton, and Coral Springs, and a blog post about "how much does a bathroom remodel cost."

That's 10 different pages ranking for 10 different searches. Each one is a door into your business. The more doors you have, the more people walk through them.

Service pages are your workhorses

A service page is a page on your website about one job you do, in the area where you do it. Each service page should focus on one thing you do. "Drain Cleaning in [City]" or "Residential Roof Replacement in [City]." The page needs a heading with the service and location, 400 to 600 words about what you do and why a homeowner would need it, pricing guidance if you're comfortable with that, photos of that specific type of work, and a quote form or click-to-call button.

Don't just list what the service is. Write about the problems it solves. A drain cleaning page should mention slow drains, standing water in showers, gurgling pipes, and sewer smell. Those are the words homeowners actually type into Google.

Location pages expand your reach

A location page, also called an area page, is a page about your trade in one specific town. If you serve five cities, you need a page for each city. "Plumber in Coral Springs" is a different search than "Plumber in Boca Raton" and Google treats them as different queries.

Each location page should mention the city name naturally throughout, reference local landmarks or neighborhoods you serve, include any city-specific information (service area boundaries, response times to that area), and link to your service pages.

The trap to avoid: don't create 20 location pages that are identical except for the city name. Google doesn't reward thin, duplicate content. Each page needs at least some unique content about why you serve that area, local context, or customer stories from that city.

Blog posts fill the gaps

Service pages target people ready to hire. Blog posts target people earlier in the process. "How much does it cost to fix a slab leak" or "signs your AC needs to be replaced" bring in homeowners who are researching. They might not hire you today, but they'll remember your name when they're ready.

Blog posts also build your site's authority in Google's eyes. A website with 20 pages of useful, relevant content ranks better across the board than a website with 3 pages. Google sees you as an authority on the topic.

You don't have to build it all at once

Start with your top five services as dedicated pages. Add two or three location pages for your most important markets. Write one blog post every couple weeks. In three months you'll have a 15 to 20 page website that works dramatically harder than a one-pager.

The site that does best usually isn't the fanciest. It's the one with the most relevant pages targeting the searches homeowners actually make.
