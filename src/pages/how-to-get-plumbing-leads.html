How to Get More Plumbing Leads (Without Cold Calling)

Cold calling homeowners is a waste of time for plumbers. Here are 6 ways to get plumbing leads that actually turn into booked jobs.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 10, 2026Updated November 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

The best way for a plumber to get more jobs without cold calling or buying shared leads is to own the places homeowners already look: a fully filled-out Google Business Profile, a website with a page for each service, a steady flow of Google reviews, referrals from other trades, and helpful answers in neighborhood groups. When those calls come in, respond fast, with an automatic text if you can't pick up. It takes a few months to build, but the work that comes in this way is yours alone.

No homeowner wants a cold call from a plumber, and no plumber wants to make one either. If you're sitting at your kitchen table at 7 PM dialing through a list from Whitepages, there are better ways to spend that time.

Plumbers who keep their schedules full usually aren't doing anything complicated. They've built a few systems that bring work to them, and they respond fast when those calls come in, which is what people mean by speed-to-lead: how quickly you get back to someone after they reach out.

Google Business Profile is your best free tool

If your GBP isn't optimized, stop reading and go do that first. When someone searches "plumber near me," Google shows three businesses in the Map Pack before any organic results. Getting into those three spots is worth more than any ad you'll ever run.

What "optimized" actually means: every service you offer listed individually (not "plumbing services" but "drain cleaning," "water heater installation," "sewer line repair," each as its own entry). Real photos of your work, your truck, your crew. Your service area set correctly. Posts published at least every couple weeks, plus a steady flow of reviews.

The simplest way to grow your review count is to text every customer a review link the day after the job, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

Your website needs to rank for specific services

"Plumber in [city]" is competitive. You probably won't rank for it right away. But "tankless water heater installation [city]" or "sewer camera inspection [city]" are way less competitive and those searchers are ready to buy.

Build a page for each service you offer. Each page needs at least 400 to 500 words, a clear heading with the service and location, a description of what you do and why a homeowner would need it, a ballpark on pricing if you're comfortable sharing, and a call-to-action to get a quote.

Over time, those pages can start ranking, and each one becomes another way for a homeowner to find you. Eight or ten service pages give you eight or ten chances to show up for a search from someone who's ready to hire.

Referrals from other trades

Plumbers work alongside electricians, HVAC techs, general contractors, and home inspectors. Those people get asked "do you know a good plumber?" constantly. If you're not building relationships with other tradespeople in your market, you're missing easy, high-quality referrals.

This doesn't require anything formal. Buy lunch for a few GCs and home inspectors. Leave a stack of cards with the electrician you always see on job sites. When someone sends you a referral, send them one back. Over time, referrals can become a steady share of your work, and they tend to close more easily than cold internet requests because trust is already built.

Online reviews are your 24/7 sales team

A homeowner comparing two plumbers will usually pick the one with 150 five-star reviews over the one with 9 reviews, even if the second plumber is better at the actual work. That's how people make decisions now.

The key is making it easy with what we call the review routine: a text with your Google review link after every job, one reminder if they don't respond, and a reply to every review that comes in. After every job, send an automated text with your Google review link. Something like: "Thanks for choosing us. If you've got 30 seconds, a review would help us out a lot: [link]." Send a reminder 48 hours later if they haven't clicked.

Don't overthink the wording. Don't offer incentives for reviews (Google doesn't allow that). Just ask consistently and the numbers stack up.

Nextdoor and Facebook groups

This is free and weirdly underused by plumbers. Join every neighborhood Facebook group and Nextdoor area in your service zone. When someone posts "anyone know a good plumber?" you want your name in that thread.

You don't need to spam or self-promote. Just answer questions when they come up, mention what you do when it's relevant, and be helpful. Homeowners check the profiles of people who recommend businesses. If your profile links back to your website, that's a potential customer.

About 10 minutes a day scanning for relevant posts is enough, and it costs nothing but your time.

Stop paying for shared leads

A shared lead is a homeowner request that a lead marketplace sends to several plumbers at once, so you're paying for a request that your competitors are calling too.

Compare that to a lead who found your website on Google, read your reviews, looked at your photos, and filled out your quote form. That person already picked you. They're just waiting for you to confirm, so those requests are far easier to close.

The investment to get there is a good website, an optimized GBP, and consistent content. It takes a few months to build up. Once it's running, you're no longer paying per request, and the work that comes in is yours alone.
