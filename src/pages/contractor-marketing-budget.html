How Much Should a Contractor Spend on Marketing in 2027?

Most contractors either spend too much or nothing on marketing. Here's how to figure out the right budget based on your revenue and growth goals.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 18, 20265 min read

I had a conversation last month with a painting contractor doing about $400,000 a year in revenue. He was spending zero on marketing. All referrals. He wanted to grow to $600,000 but couldn't figure out how to get more work without just waiting for his phone to ring.

The answer was boring: he needed to spend money on marketing. The question was how much.

The standard rule and why it's only half right

The common advice is 5 to 10 percent of revenue. For a contractor doing $500,000 a year, that's $25,000 to $50,000 annually, or roughly $2,000 to $4,000 a month.

That range is a starting point, but the right number depends on where you are in your business. A new contractor trying to build a customer base needs to spend more aggressively, maybe 10 to 15 percent, because they're building awareness from zero. An established contractor with strong referrals and a full calendar might only need 3 to 5 percent to maintain and grow incrementally.

Where the money should go

The mistake most contractors make isn't the total budget. It's the allocation. Spending $3,000 a month on Google Ads with a website that doesn't convert and no follow-up system is burning cash.

Here's how I'd split a $2,000 monthly marketing budget for a contractor who's starting from scratch on the digital side.

Website: $80 to $200 a month for a managed site with lead tools. This is the foundation. Without a site that converts, everything else is wasted.

Google Business Profile: $0. It's free. Spend the time to optimize it properly and maintain it weekly.

Content (blog posts, service pages): $0 to $500 a month. You can write this yourself if you have the time, or pay someone to write it. Two to four posts a month is enough.

Google Ads: $500 to $1,000 a month if you need leads now. This is the accelerator while your organic presence builds. Once organic kicks in, you can dial ads down.

Review management software: $50 to $100 a month for automated review requests. Worth every penny.

CRM and follow-up automation: $100 to $200 a month. This is what prevents you from wasting the leads you generate.

That totals $730 to $2,000 depending on what you need. The range is wide because a contractor with a good website and strong reviews might skip ads entirely while a new contractor might lean heavily on them.

Tracking what works

The biggest waste in contractor marketing is spending money without tracking results. If you can't tell whether your $1,500 in Google Ads last month produced $15,000 in jobs or $0, you're guessing.

At minimum, track these things: how many leads came from your website form, how many calls came from your GBP listing, how many leads you followed up with and how many you booked. If you're running ads, track cost per lead and cost per booked job.

If a marketing channel isn't producing leads that turn into jobs at a reasonable cost, cut it and put that money somewhere else. I've seen contractors spend $1,200 a month on a print ad in a local magazine because they've "always done it." When they tracked the results for three months, it produced two calls and zero jobs. That $3,600 would have been better spent on their website and GBP.

The free stuff that compounds

Some of the highest-ROI marketing for contractors is free. Responding to leads within two minutes (instead of two hours) costs nothing but a text. Asking every customer for a Google review costs nothing but 10 seconds. Posting a job site photo on Facebook costs nothing. Answering a question in a neighborhood group costs nothing.

These free activities don't replace a marketing budget, but they multiply its effectiveness. A $1,000 ad spend produces more revenue when your reviews are strong, your website converts, and your follow-up is fast.

When to increase your budget

If you're consistently closing 25 percent or more of your leads, your cost per job is reasonable for your trade, and you want more work, increase your budget. You've proven the system works and more input produces more output.

If you're getting plenty of leads but only closing 10 percent, don't increase your ad spend. Fix your follow-up process, your website conversion, or your estimate-to-close rate. Spending more on a leaky funnel just means you're leaking faster.

The right marketing budget is the one that produces enough leads to hit your revenue goal, at a cost per job that leaves you profitable. For most contractors doing $300,000 to $1,000,000 in annual revenue, that's somewhere between $1,000 and $4,000 a month when it's allocated properly.
