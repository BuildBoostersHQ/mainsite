How Much Does a Contractor Website Cost in 2026?

DIY, one-time build or monthly plan? Here is what each one really costs, what drives the price up, and how to tell if a quote is fair.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 12, 2026Updated November 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

A contractor website usually lands in one of three price ranges. A DIY builder runs about $20 to $50 a month plus your own time. A custom build from a freelancer or agency is usually a few thousand dollars up front, plus hosting and paid edits. A monthly plan typically runs $150 to $500 a month and bundles the site, hosting, updates and often lead tools. The right pick depends less on the price tag and more on whether the site actually brings in jobs.

If you've ever asked three people what a website costs, you probably got three answers that weren't even close. That's because "a website" can mean a one-page template you set up on a Sunday, or a full system with service pages, quote forms and automatic follow-up. Here's how the pricing really breaks down for contractors, and how to figure out what you should be paying.

The three ways contractors pay for a website

1. Do it yourself

Website builders like Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy let you build your own site for roughly $20 to $50 a month. The software is cheap. Your time isn't. Expect to spend a few weekends writing copy, picking photos, and figuring out why the form isn't sending emails.

Good fit if: you're just starting out, you enjoy this kind of thing, and you mostly need something to point people to.

2. A one-time custom build

A freelancer or agency builds the site for a flat fee. For a contractor site with a handful of service pages, quotes commonly land somewhere between $2,000 and $10,000, and bigger custom projects go well past that. After launch you'll usually pay separately for hosting, and edits are often billed by the hour.

Good fit if: you want something fully custom, you have the cash up front, and you have someone to maintain it after launch.

3. A monthly plan

A monthly website plan is one where you pay a monthly fee that covers the build, hosting, updates and support. Plans typically run $150 to $500 a month. The better ones also include the tools that turn visitors into jobs, like missed-call text-back, review requests and a single inbox for leads.

Good fit if: you'd rather not pay a big lump sum, and you want someone else handling changes and keeping it running.

What makes the price go up

  • Number of pages. A page for each service and each town you work in helps you show up in more searches, but each one takes time to write and build.
  • Copywriting. Writing that sounds like you and answers what homeowners ask takes work. Templates are cheaper but read like everyone else's.
  • Photos. Real photos of your jobs beat stock photos every time. Someone has to collect, edit and place them.
  • Lead tools. Quote forms, online booking, chat, call tracking and CRM connections all add setup time.
  • Ongoing work. Updates, new pages, security, backups and SEO are either included or billed extra.

The costs nobody puts in the quote

  • Your domain. Usually a small yearly fee. Make sure it's registered in your name, not the developer's.
  • Edits after launch. "Can you swap this photo?" at an hourly rate adds up fast.
  • Plugins and apps. Booking tools, form tools and review widgets often have their own monthly fees.
  • A site that doesn't convert. The most expensive website is the one that looks fine but never makes the phone ring.
Quick math: if your average job is $1,500, one extra job a month covers most monthly website plans several times over. That's the number to judge a website by, not the sticker price.

What a contractor website should include

  • Click-to-call and a short quote form at the top of every page, especially on mobile
  • A page for each main service you offer
  • Pages for the towns or areas you serve
  • Real photos of your work and your crew
  • Your Google reviews, shown where people are deciding
  • License, insurance and how long you've been in business
  • Fast load times on a phone
  • Instant follow-up when someone calls, texts or fills out the form

Questions to ask before you sign anything

  1. Who owns the domain and the content? You should, no matter what.
  2. Is there a contract, and how long is it? Long lock-ins are a red flag if the site isn't working.
  3. Are edits included? Get it in writing.
  4. What happens if I leave? Can you take your domain, your content and your photos with you?
  5. How will this get me more jobs? If the answer is only about design, keep asking.

The bottom line

Price matters, but it's the wrong first question. Start with what the site needs to do for your business: get found, look trustworthy, and make it easy to call or ask for a quote. Then pick the option that does that for a price that makes sense against your average job.

How much should a small contractor spend on a website?

It depends on how much one job is worth to you. Many small contractors do well with a monthly plan in the $150 to $500 range, because it spreads out the cost and includes updates. Judge it by how many jobs it brings in, not just the price.

Is a monthly website plan worth it?

It can be, if it includes hosting, edits and the tools that turn visitors into leads. Make sure you own your domain and there is no long contract.

How long does it take to build a contractor website?

A simple site can go live in a week or two once you have your logo, photos and business details. Bigger custom builds can take a month or more.

Can I just use my Facebook page instead of a website?

Facebook helps, but you don't control it, and it rarely shows up when someone searches Google for your service in your town. A website gives you a home base you own.
