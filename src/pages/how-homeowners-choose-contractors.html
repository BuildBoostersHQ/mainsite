How Homeowners Choose a Contractor: The Decision Journey

Homeowners don't spend weeks researching contractors. They search on their phone, look at a few names, read the reviews and reach out to whoever looks right, often in the same sitting.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 24, 20266 min read
The short answer

Homeowners usually choose a contractor in five quick stages: they search on their phone, build a short list of three or fewer businesses, check reviews, reach out to the one that looks best, and book whoever responds first and makes it easy. Many of them make that decision in under 30 minutes, so you need to show up on Google, have recent reviews and real photos, and answer fast even when you can't pick up the phone.

From the contractor's side, it can feel random. One week the phone rings nonstop, the next it's quiet, and a homeowner who seemed ready to sign goes with someone else. The way homeowners pick a contractor is actually pretty predictable, though, and once you understand the steps they go through, it's much easier to see where you're winning jobs and where you're losing them.

Stage 1: The search starts on a phone

The journey starts when something breaks, leaks, stops working or simply gets old enough that the homeowner can't ignore it anymore. Their first move is usually a search like "plumber near me" or "roof repair in [town]", and BrightLocal found that 73% of local searches start on a mobile device. That means your website has to load quickly and work well on a small screen, with a phone number they can tap and a quote form that's easy to fill out with one thumb.

At this stage you're not competing on price or quality yet. You're competing on whether you show up at all. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that there's no way to pay for a better spot in the map results. A complete Google Business Profile and a website with a page for each service and town you cover give you the best shot.

Stage 2: The short list

Homeowners don't call ten contractors. According to BrightLocal, 72% look at three or fewer businesses before deciding, and 42% of searchers click on the Google map pack results. In practice, your real competition is the two or three other names sitting next to yours on that screen. If you're not in the map pack or near the top of the results, you probably never make the short list, and nothing else on this page matters for that customer.

Stage 3: Reviews decide who gets the call

Once they have a short list, homeowners read reviews. The BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses and 41% always do. Reviews are also where a lot of contractors get crossed off, since 65% of people who rejected a business did it for a review-related reason like a low rating, bad reviews, or reviews that were missing or old.

This is the stage where your photos and website do their work too. A homeowner who sees real pictures of your trucks, your crew and finished jobs has a much easier time trusting you than one looking at stock photos. If you want more reviews coming in, start with a simple review routine, and for the photo side, see our guide to the best photos for a contractor website.

Stage 4: The first contact

When the homeowner finally reaches out, they call, text, fill out a form or start a chat, and this is where a lot of jobs quietly slip away. In the CallRail survey, 78% of consumers said they had abandoned a business after an unanswered call, 21% said they immediately call another business, and only 42% said they leave a voicemail. A contractor who is on a roof when the phone rings can lose the job without ever knowing the customer called.

An automatic text back the moment you miss a call keeps that homeowner talking to you instead of dialing the next name. Our article on what missed calls really cost contractors walks through the math, and you can see the setup in what missed-call text-back is.

Stage 5: A fast decision and a booked job

The final stage moves quickly. BrightLocal found that 28% of consumers decide in under five minutes and 75% decide in under 30 minutes. For a contractor, that means the homeowner often books whoever responds first with a clear next step, like a time for an estimate or a link to pick one. If an estimate goes out and the customer goes quiet, a friendly follow-up text a few days later brings a surprising amount of that work back. There's a full sequence in our guide to following up without being annoying.

Where contractors lose homeowners

If you look at the table, you'll notice that the stages where contractors lose people aren't usually about the quality of the work. They're about being invisible on Google, having too few recent reviews, or not answering fast enough. Those are fixable problems, and each one has a simple fix:

  • Not showing up. If you're missing from the map results, homeowners never see you in the first place.
  • Old or thin reviews. A profile with a handful of reviews from three years ago looks risky next to one with fresh reviews every month.
  • Slow or no response. A missed call with no text back sends the homeowner straight to the next contractor on their list.
  • A hard-to-use website. If the phone number is buried or the page takes forever on mobile, people leave. Our guide to a good contractor website covers what to fix.

The bottom line

Homeowners choose a contractor quickly, from a very short list, mostly based on what they see on their phone. If you show up on Google, have recent reviews and real photos, and respond the moment someone reaches out, you'll be the obvious choice at every stage.
