What Is Missed-Call Text-Back? How It Works for Contractors

Missed-call text-back sends an automatic text to every caller you can't pick up for, so here is how it works, how it compares to the other options and what it takes to set it up the right way.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 24, 20268 min read
The short answer

Missed-call text-back is an automation that sends a text message from your business phone number to anyone whose call you don't answer, usually within a few seconds of the call ending. The text tells the caller you got their call and asks what they need, so they can explain the job by text instead of hanging up and calling the next contractor. For contractors who are on a job site and can't pick up, it turns a missed call into a text conversation you can answer between jobs.

If you run a crew, you already know the problem. The phone rings while you're on a roof, under a house or in the middle of a quote with another customer, and by the time you check it, the caller has moved on. Missed-call text-back is one of the simplest ways to keep that person talking to you, and it works whether you miss one call a week or twenty.

What missed-call text-back is

Missed-call text-back is a feature of a business phone system that watches for unanswered calls and replies to each one with an automatic text. The text comes from the same number the customer just dialed, so it looks like it came from you, because it did. You write the message once, and the system sends it every time a call goes unanswered, day or night.

I call the three-step flow behind it the missed-call loop, which means a missed call triggers an automatic text, the customer replies to that text, and the conversation lands back in your inbox so you can pick it up. The loop is what makes it work, because the call doesn't just end in silence or a voicemail greeting, it comes back around to you with the customer still on the line by text.

How missed-call text-back works

Here is the missed-call loop, step by step, from the customer's side and yours:

  1. A call goes unanswered. A homeowner calls your business number, and you can't pick up because you're working, driving or it's after hours.
  2. Your number texts them back within seconds. The system sees the missed call and sends your auto-text, something like "Sorry we missed your call, we're on a job right now, what can we help you with?"
  3. The customer replies and you pick it up from one inbox. They text back with what they need, and you answer between jobs, send a booking link or call them back when you're free, with the whole conversation saved in one place.

The customer doesn't have to download anything or sign up for anything. From their side, they called a business, missed the person, and got a friendly text a few seconds later. Missed-call text-back vs. voicemail, answering services and AI call agents

Missed-call text-back isn't the only way to handle calls you can't pick up. Answering services and AI call agents are real options with real strengths, and a lot of businesses use more than one. Here is a fair look at how they compare:

If you get a lot of emergency calls at night, an answering service or AI agent can make sense alongside it, so the job you can't afford to miss gets handled live.

How to set it up

Whoever you use, a good setup comes down to the same five pieces:

  1. Use a business number that can text. Your missed-call texts and your replies should all come from one number your customers recognize, which usually means moving your number to a phone system that supports texting or forwarding calls through a new business number.
  2. Register it for business texting. In the U.S., business texts from a local number go through a registration process called A2P 10DLC, and toll-free numbers have a similar verification step, so your texts actually get delivered instead of filtered by the carriers.
  3. Write the message in your own voice. Keep it short and friendly, put your business name in it, and ask an easy question so the customer knows exactly how to reply.
  4. Add an after-hours version. Callers at night and on weekends should get a message that tells them when you'll get back to them, so they aren't waiting for a reply that won't come until morning.
  5. Keep every conversation in one inbox. Calls, texts, form fills and chat messages should all land in one place, so nothing slips through while you're on a job.

Here is an example of a daytime message you could start with:

Hi, this is [Business Name], sorry we missed your call! We're on a job right now. What can we help you with? Text back here and we'll get right back to you.

Here is an after-hours version:

Thanks for calling [Business Name]! We're closed for the night, but we'll get back to you first thing in the morning. Feel free to text us what you need and a good time to reach you.

Will missed-call text-back work with my current phone number?

It often can, depending on your phone setup. Some contractors move their existing number to a system that supports texting, while others forward calls through a new business number, and either way the texts go out from the number your customers call.

Is it legal to text someone who just called my business?

Replying to someone who just called you is generally fine, but business texting in the U.S. needs to be registered through A2P 10DLC or toll-free verification, and you should always honor opt-out replies like STOP.

How fast does the text go out?

A good setup sends the text within a few seconds of the call ending, while the customer still has their phone in their hand. You can test it on our demo line at (786) 933-8832 to see the timing for yourself.

Does missed-call text-back replace answering the phone?

It doesn't replace it, and it isn't meant to. You should still answer when you can, and the auto-text covers the calls you can't get to, so nobody hangs up without hearing from you.

What should a missed-call text say?

Keep it short, use your business name, say you're on a job or closed for the day, and ask what they need. A friendly question gives the customer an easy reason to reply instead of calling someone else.
