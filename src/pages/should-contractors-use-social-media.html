Should Contractors Use Social Media? (Honest Answer)

Social media for contractors is overrated unless you do it right. Here's what works, what doesn't, and where to actually spend your time.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 9, 20265 min read

Every marketing person will tell you contractors need to be on social media. Post three times a week, use Reels, engage with your audience, build your brand. That advice sounds great until you're a one-truck operation trying to post content at 9 PM after a 12-hour day.

Here's what I actually think: social media can help contractors, but it's the third or fourth thing you should focus on, and most of the advice out there is a waste of your time.

What matters more than social media

If your Google Business Profile is neglected, your website doesn't convert, and you have no follow-up system for leads, posting on Instagram isn't going to fix your business. Those three things directly generate and close leads. Social media mostly generates awareness, which is valuable but further from the sale.

Get your GBP optimized. Get a website that actually converts visitors into calls. Set up automated follow-up for your leads. After all three of those are running, then social media makes sense as a layer on top.

The one platform worth your time

If you're going to pick one platform as a contractor, make it Facebook. I know that sounds old-school. But Facebook has two things Instagram and TikTok don't: local community groups and an older demographic that actually hires contractors.

When someone in a neighborhood Facebook group posts "need a painter, any recommendations?" and three people tag your business, that's a warm lead who already has social proof. You can't buy that with an Instagram Reel.

Join every community group in your service area. Don't spam. When someone asks for a recommendation in your trade, reply with your info. When someone posts a question you can answer, answer it. That takes 10 minutes a day and produces better leads than any content calendar.

Job site photos over polished content

Contractors try to make their social media look like a marketing agency runs it. Branded templates, scripted captions, motivational quotes over sunrise photos. That stuff gets zero engagement from homeowners.

What actually works: a 15-second video of you ripping up an old deck. A before-and-after of a bathroom remodel. A photo of your crew at 7 AM heading out. These take 30 seconds to capture and they're the content that gets shared in those community groups.

Don't worry about lighting, editing, or captions. The raw, unpolished look works better for contractors because it proves you're doing real work. A homeowner scrolling through their feed can tell the difference between a staged photo and a real job site.

Instagram and TikTok: good for some, not for most

Short-form video works if you enjoy making it. Some contractors genuinely like shooting Reels and TikToks and they build a following that leads to jobs. If that's you, go for it.

But if filming yourself while explaining how to unclog a drain sounds like torture, don't force it. Bad, reluctant content is worse than no content. The homeowner looking for a plumber at 2 AM isn't scrolling TikTok for plumbing tips. They're Googling and they're looking at reviews.

The exception is high-visual trades. Hardscaping, painting, remodeling, decks, and pools photograph well and the before-and-after content performs on Instagram. If you're in one of those trades and you're already taking photos on the job, posting them to Instagram takes two minutes and can build a referral pipeline over time.

How much time is social media worth?

For most contractors: 15 to 20 minutes a day, max. Check your local Facebook groups, respond to any mentions, post a job site photo if you have one. That's it.

If you're spending an hour a day on social media and your website doesn't have service pages for each trade you do, you've got your priorities backwards. Social media is dessert. Your website and GBP are the meal.

The bottom line

Social media won't save a contracting business with bad fundamentals. But if your website converts, your GBP is strong, and your follow-up is automated, social media can add a steady stream of warm leads on top. Focus on Facebook groups and raw job site content. Skip the polished marketing agency aesthetic. Spend 15 minutes a day, not an hour.
