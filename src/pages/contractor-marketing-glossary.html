Contractor Marketing Glossary

Every marketing term you keep hearing from agencies, ad reps and software companies, explained in plain English for people who run a trade business.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 24, 202615 min read
The short answer

This glossary explains 37 contractor marketing terms in plain language, from missed-call text-back and speed-to-lead to the Map Pack, Local Services Ads and A2P 10DLC. Each definition is short, written for someone running a trade business rather than a marketing agency, and links to a longer guide when there's more to know. Jump to a letter below, or use your browser's search to find a term.

Marketing comes with a lot of jargon, and it's easy to feel like people are using it to sell you something you don't need. Knowing what the words mean makes it much easier to ask good questions, compare quotes and spot when a pitch doesn't add up.

A

A2P 10DLC

A2P 10DLC stands for application-to-person messaging over a 10-digit long code, which is the system U.S. phone carriers use for business texts sent from regular local phone numbers. Before a business can send texts from software (like automatic replies or review requests), it has to register its business and the type of messages it sends through The Campaign Registry, and carriers can block or filter texts from numbers that skip this step. Toll-free numbers go through a separate verification process instead, but the idea is the same, because carriers want to know who is texting their customers and why.

AI chat

AI chat is a chat or text assistant on your website or phone line that answers common questions in plain language, like what services you offer, which towns you cover and when you're available. A good setup collects the customer's name, number and job details, and can book an appointment or hand the conversation to you. It works best as a backup for the hours you can't get to your phone, and it should be set up so it never makes promises about prices or timelines you haven't approved.

B

Backlink

A backlink is a link from another website to yours, like your supplier's dealer page, a local news story or your chamber of commerce listing linking to your site. Google treats links from real, relevant websites as a sign that other people trust you, so a few good local links are worth more than hundreds of spammy ones. Paying for bulk links can backfire, because Google's spam policies specifically call out buying links to manipulate rankings.

Bounce rate

Bounce rate is the share of website visits where the person showed up and left without really doing anything. In Google Analytics 4 it counts visits that weren't "engaged," meaning the person stayed less than 10 seconds, looked at only one page and didn't take an action like calling or filling out a form. A high bounce rate on your homepage often points to a slow site, a confusing first screen or a phone number that's hard to find.

Back to the A to Z list

C

CTA (call to action)

A CTA, or call to action, is the button or line that tells a visitor exactly what to do next, like "Call now," "Get a free quote" or "Book a time." Every page on a contractor website should have one clear CTA near the top and another near the bottom, so nobody has to hunt for how to reach you. You can see how this fits into the rest of a site in what makes a good contractor website.

Citation

A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website, like Yelp, Angi, the BBB, Apple Maps, Bing Places or your local chamber of commerce. Citations help Google confirm that your business is real and where it operates. The details need to match your Google Business Profile exactly, which is why citations and NAP consistency usually get cleaned up together.

Click-through rate

Click-through rate, or CTR, is the percentage of people who saw your ad or search listing and actually clicked it. If your Google listing shows up 1,000 times and 50 people click, your CTR is 5%. A low CTR usually means your title, description or ad text isn't giving homeowners a reason to pick you over the next result.

Close rate

Close rate is the percentage of quotes or estimates that turn into booked jobs. If you give 10 estimates and win 3, your close rate is 30%. It's one of the most useful numbers to know, because it tells you what every missed call or slow reply is really costing you, and you can plug yours into the missed call calculator to see the yearly number.

Conversion rate

Conversion rate is the percentage of people who take the action you want, like visitors to your website who call you or fill out your quote form. If 200 people visit your site in a month and 8 of them reach out, your website's conversion rate is 4%. Improving it is often cheaper than buying more traffic, and the usual fixes are a faster site, a clear CTA and real reviews on the page, which this guide on websites that don't bring in calls covers in more detail.

Cost per acquisition

Cost per acquisition, or CPA, is how much you spend in marketing to win one paying customer, not just one phone call. If you spend $1,000 on ads in a month and book 4 jobs from them, your CPA is $250. It's a more honest number than cost per lead, because it includes every request that never turned into work, and it helps you set a marketing budget that makes sense for your average job size.

Cost per lead

Cost per lead is what you pay for each new customer inquiry, like a call, a form or a message, whether or not it turns into a job. Pay-per-request services and ads are often compared on this number, but a cheap request that five other contractors also got is not really cheap. You can run your own numbers with the shared lead cost calculator or compare options in Angi and HomeAdvisor alternatives.

Back to the A to Z list

D

Domain authority

Domain authority is a 1 to 100 score made by the SEO company Moz that tries to predict how well a website might rank based mostly on the links pointing to it. Google doesn't use this score, so it isn't a ranking factor, and other tools have their own versions with different names. It's fine as a rough way to compare your site to competitors, but it isn't something to chase for its own sake.

Drip campaign

A drip campaign is a series of texts or emails that go out automatically on a schedule, like a check-in two days after a quote, a reminder a week later and a seasonal tune-up offer a few months after that. The point is to stay in touch without you having to remember every person. It works best when the messages are short, personal and stop as soon as the customer replies or books.

E

E-E-A-T

E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness, and it comes from the guidelines Google gives the people who rate the quality of its search results. It isn't a single score or a direct ranking factor, but it describes the kind of content Google wants to show. For a contractor, that means real photos of your work, your license and insurance information, real reviews and pages written by someone who actually knows the trade.

Back to the A to Z list

F

Follow-up sequence

A follow-up sequence is a planned set of messages you send after someone reaches out or gets a quote, so nobody falls through the cracks because you got busy. A simple one might be a text right away, another the next day, a check-in after the quote and a last friendly message a week later. You can copy a full example from this 6-text follow-up plan.

Back to the A to Z list

G

Google Ads

Google Ads is Google's advertising platform, where you pay to show up in the sponsored results at the top of Google searches. The classic search ads work on a pay-per-click basis, so you set a budget and pay when someone clicks your ad, whether or not they call. Google Ads also runs Local Services Ads, which work differently and charge per qualified inquiry instead of per click.

Google Business Profile (GBP)

Your Google Business Profile, or GBP, is the free listing that shows your business on Google Search and Google Maps, with your hours, phone number, photos, service area and reviews. It's what decides whether you can show up in the Map Pack, and it's free to claim and manage. You can learn how to set one up and keep it active in Google Business Profile for contractors.

Back to the A to Z list

L

Lead nurture

Lead nurture means staying in touch with people who reached out but aren't ready to book yet, like the homeowner who wants a new roof next spring. Instead of letting them go cold, you send helpful, low-pressure messages until they're ready, so you're the contractor they remember. It's usually done with a drip campaign or a follow-up sequence.

Local SEO

Local SEO is the work of helping your business show up when people nearby search for what you do, like "plumber near me" or "roof repair in Tampa." It covers your Google Business Profile, reviews, your website's service and area pages, and your listings on other sites. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, so local SEO focuses on the parts you can control, and you can read more in how to get found on Google without ads.

Local Services Ads (LSAs)

Local Services Ads, or LSAs, are the Google ads for service businesses that usually show at the very top of the search results, with a "Google Verified" badge, which replaced the older Google Guaranteed and Google Screened badges in 2025. Instead of paying per click, you pay for each qualified call, message or booking that comes from the ad, and you can dispute ones that aren't valid. To qualify, your business has to pass Google's screening, which can include license, insurance and background checks depending on your trade and location.

Back to the A to Z list

M

Map Pack

The Map Pack is the box of three local businesses with a map that shows up near the top of Google when someone searches for a nearby service. It gets a big share of the clicks, and BrightLocal's 2026 research found that 42% of searchers click on map pack results. Your spot in it comes from your Google Business Profile, not from paying Google, and this guide to Google Maps ranking explains what helps.

Missed-call text-back

Missed-call text-back is an automatic text that goes out to anyone whose call you miss, usually within a few seconds, so they hear from you before they dial the next contractor. The text says something like "Sorry we missed you, what can we help with?" and the customer can reply right there. You can read how it works in what is missed-call text-back and what those missed calls add up to in what missed calls really cost contractors.

Back to the A to Z list

N

NAP consistency

NAP stands for name, address and phone number, and NAP consistency means those details are exactly the same everywhere your business is listed online. If your Google profile says "Ruiz Roofing LLC" with one phone number and Yelp has an old address and a different number, it can confuse both customers and Google. Pick one version of each and use it on your website, your Google profile and every citation.

Back to the A to Z list

O

One inbox (unified inbox)

One inbox, also called a unified inbox, puts your texts, emails, website chats and Facebook and Instagram messages in a single place, usually an app on your phone. Instead of checking five apps and forgetting to answer one, you see every message together and can reply from the same screen. It makes fast replies much easier, which matters a lot for speed-to-lead.

Online booking

Online booking lets customers pick a time for an estimate or service call from a calendar on your website, without having to call or wait for a callback. You control which days and hours are open, and the system can send reminders so fewer people forget the appointment. It's especially handy for the people searching late at night who'd rather book than leave a voicemail.

Organic search

Organic search means the regular, unpaid results on Google, as opposed to the sponsored ads at the top. You can't pay Google to rank higher in them, so showing up there comes from having a solid website, a complete Google Business Profile and good reviews. It takes longer to build than ads, but those visits don't cost you anything per click, which this guide on getting found without ads explains.

Back to the A to Z list

P

Paid search (PPC)

Paid search is advertising that shows up in search results, and PPC stands for pay-per-click, because you pay each time someone clicks your ad. Google Ads is the biggest example for contractors, and you choose the searches, towns and daily budget you want. The upside is that it can bring in calls quickly, while the downside is that the calls stop when you stop paying, so it works best alongside a strong website and organic search presence.

Back to the A to Z list

Q

Quote form

A quote form is the short form on your website where a customer types their name, phone number, address and what they need so you can follow up with a price or an estimate. Shorter forms get filled out more often, so ask only for what you really need to call them back. A good setup texts or emails you the moment a form comes in, so you can reply quickly.

Back to the A to Z list

R

Retargeting (remarketing)

Retargeting, also called remarketing, means showing ads to people who already visited your website but didn't call or book. They might see your ad later on Facebook, Instagram, YouTube or other websites, which reminds them of you when they're ready to hire. It usually costs less than reaching brand-new people, because these homeowners already know who you are.

Reputation management

Reputation management is everything you do to earn and protect a good name online, mainly getting a steady stream of new reviews and replying to the ones you get. It also means handling bad reviews calmly and reporting fake ones that break Google's rules. You can't delete honest reviews you don't like, so the best protection is having plenty of recent, real ones, which this guide to getting more Google reviews walks through.

Review gating

Review gating is the practice of screening customers first, like asking "How did we do?" and then only sending the Google review link to the happy ones. Google's review policy doesn't allow selectively asking for positive reviews, and gated reviews can be removed. The safe approach is to ask every customer the same way, which is covered in how to get more Google reviews.

Review request automation

Review request automation sends a review request text or email automatically after a job is marked done, usually with a direct link to your Google review page and one friendly reminder if they don't respond. It solves the biggest review problem, which is simply forgetting to ask on busy weeks. It has to ask every customer the same way to stay within Google's rules on review gating.

Back to the A to Z list

S

Schema markup

Schema markup is a bit of behind-the-scenes code on your website that tells Google facts about your business in a format it can read easily, like your business type, phone number, hours, service area and FAQs. Visitors never see it, but it helps search engines understand your pages. It doesn't guarantee better rankings, but it's a standard part of a well-built site, and it's on the contractor website checklist. You can write your own with our free contractor schema generator.

Service area pages

Service area pages are pages on your website for each town or city you work in, like "Roof Repair in Coral Gables" or "Plumber in Boca Raton." They help you show up when people search for your trade in a specific town, and each one should have real details about that area instead of the same text with the city name swapped. You can read why they matter in why your contractor website needs more pages.

Service pages

Service pages are separate pages on your website for each main job you do, like roof replacement, roof repair and gutter installation, instead of one page listing everything. Each page can explain that service, show photos and answer common questions, which helps Google match you to more specific searches. They also help homeowners, because someone who needs a water heater replaced lands right on the page about water heaters.

Speed-to-lead

Speed-to-lead is how fast you reply to a new call, text or form after someone reaches out. The faster you respond, the better your chances, and a Harvard Business Review study found that companies who replied within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a meaningful conversation with the decision maker as those who waited even an hour longer. You can see how to reply in under a minute, even from a job site, in speed to lead for contractors.

Back to the A to Z list

T

Tap-to-call

Tap-to-call is a phone number or button on your website that starts a call with one tap on a phone, instead of making people copy and type the number. BrightLocal's 2026 research found that 73% of local searches start on mobile, so this matters on every page. It should sit near the top of the screen where people can find it without scrolling.

The bottom line

You don't need to speak fluent marketing to run a busy trade business, but knowing these terms helps you ask better questions and tell a useful pitch from a fancy-sounding one. If someone uses a word you don't know, ask them to explain it in plain English, and if they can't, that tells you something too.

What does a contractor really need from all of these terms?

You don't need to master every term on this page. The ones that move the needle for most local contractors are a complete Google Business Profile, a steady stream of reviews, a website with clear service and area pages, and a fast reply to every call and message, including an automatic text when you miss a call.

What's the difference between Google Ads and Local Services Ads?

Regular Google Ads search ads charge you each time someone clicks your ad, whether or not they reach out. Local Services Ads usually appear above them, show a Google Verified badge, and charge for each qualified call, message or booking instead of per click, after your business passes Google's screening.

Is cost per lead or cost per acquisition more important?

Cost per acquisition is the better number for running your business, because it tells you what you actually spend to win a paying job. Cost per lead is useful for comparing sources, but a cheap request that never books, or one that went to five other contractors too, can end up costing more per job.

Do I have to register my business to send automatic texts?

If you send texts from software using a regular local number in the U.S., carriers expect that number to be registered through A2P 10DLC, and toll-free numbers need their own verification. Unregistered business texts can be filtered or blocked, so registration is part of setting up missed-call text-back or review request texts properly.
