Do Contractors Really Need a Website in 2027?

Some contractors think a Facebook page is enough. Here's why you still need a website in 2027 and what it actually costs to get one that generates leads.

By Madeleine JolieOctober 13, 20265 min read

I hear this one a lot. "I get all my work from referrals and word of mouth. Why do I need a website?"

And honestly, if you're booked solid for the next six months from referrals alone, maybe you don't. But I'd push back on that for a few reasons.

Referrals look you up online too

Even when someone gets your name from a neighbor, the first thing they do is Google you. If nothing comes up, or if all they find is a bare Facebook page with your last post from 2024, some of them will keep searching and find a competitor instead.

A website doesn't replace referrals. It makes them stick. When someone Googles "[your company name]" and finds a clean site with your services, your photos, and 85 five-star reviews, that referral turns into a booked job almost every time.

Without a website, some percentage of those referrals quietly look elsewhere. You'll never know how many because they just never called.

Facebook isn't a website

Facebook business pages are free and easy, and that's about all they have going for them. The problems with relying on Facebook as your web presence: you don't control it (Facebook changes what people see based on their algorithm), you can't rank on Google with a Facebook page the way you can with a real website, your posts reach maybe 5 percent of your followers unless you pay to boost them, and it looks unprofessional compared to competitors who have actual sites.

A Facebook page is fine as a supplement. As your only online presence, it tells potential customers you either can't afford a website or don't take your business seriously enough to have one. Neither of those messages books jobs.

Google is where homeowners find contractors now

97 percent of people search online for local services. That's not some made-up marketing stat. That's how people actually behave. When someone's water heater breaks at 6 AM, they grab their phone and search "plumber near me" or "water heater repair [city]." If you don't have a website, you're invisible in that search.

A Google Business Profile helps and it's free. But the contractors who rank highest in local search have a GBP connected to a website with service pages, location pages, and content. Google uses your website to understand what you do and where you do it. Without one, you're leaving that context gap for Google to fill in with your competitors.

What a contractor website actually costs

This is where people get nervous because they've heard quotes of $5,000 or $10,000 for a custom site. Here's the reality for contractors: you don't need a custom-built masterpiece. You need a site that loads fast on mobile, says what you do and where, has your phone number front and center, shows your reviews and your work, and makes it easy to request a quote.

That can cost anywhere from $500 to $2,000 for a one-time build, or $79 to $200 a month for a managed service that includes hosting, updates, and lead tools. The monthly model works well for contractors because someone else handles the tech and you get tools like missed-call text-back and automated follow-up built in.

The question isn't really "can I afford a website." It's "can I afford to keep losing the leads I'd get if I had one."

When you can skip the website (for now)

If you're a one-person operation doing side jobs on weekends and you get enough work from your personal network, a website genuinely isn't a priority. Spend your money on tools and materials.

But the moment you're trying to grow, to hire someone, to fill your calendar consistently, to stop relying on whoever happens to know someone who knows you, you need a website. It's the foundation everything else builds on. Ads, SEO, review marketing, follow-up systems. All of it assumes you have a place to send people.
