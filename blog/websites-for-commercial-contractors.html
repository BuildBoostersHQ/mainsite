What Generic Marketing Misses About Commercial Contracting

Commercial contracting does not work like residential. The buyer is not emotional or in a hurry. They are comparing bids, checking insurance certificates, calling references, and running background checks. A marketing company that treats commercial like residential misses how the sale actually works.

The sales cycle is longer. A tenant buildout inquiry today might not start construction for three months. The follow-up has to stay alive over weeks, not minutes. If your system sends one text and stops, the lead goes cold.

The buyer is different. Property managers and facilities directors search differently than homeowners. They type "commercial contractor [city]" or "tenant buildout contractor near me." They look at your site for sixty seconds, checking for project types, insurance, and whether you look like a company that handles their scale of work.

Recurring work matters more than one-off projects. A single property management company can feed you maintenance work for years. Your site and your follow-up need to support that relationship.

We build for contractors. Commercial is one of the trades we know, alongside remodelers, electricians, and the rest of the construction trades.

Local SEO for commercial contractors is different from residential. The search volume is lower, but the project value is higher. Ranking for "commercial contractor [city]" or "tenant buildout [city]" might bring in two leads a month, but each one could be a six-figure project.

Every RFQ and Every Call Answered, Even When You Are on a Job Site

Where Google Ads, Bid Boards and Your Past Clients Fit

Google Ads. You pay for clicks at the top of the results. Commercial keywords are expensive — $20 to $50 per click in many markets. It can bring RFQs quickly, and it stops the day you stop paying.

Bid boards and lead services. Services like BuildZoom, iSqFt, or Dodge send you project leads. Some are worth the subscription, some sell the same lead to every contractor in town. Know what you are paying for and track your close rate.

Your past clients. One-click campaigns go out to every property manager, GC, and facilities director you have ever worked for. A maintenance reminder or a "we have availability in Q1" message turns your project history into recurring work and referrals. Past clients are the most valuable pipeline any commercial contractor has.

Reviews for Commercial Contractors

Reviews work differently in commercial. You will not get 50 reviews a year the way a residential roofer does. But the reviews you do get carry more weight, because property managers and GCs read them carefully and there are fewer to compare.

The challenge is that commercial clients are busy professionals. They are not going to remember to leave a review on their own. So we make it easy: after the project the client gets a text with a link. One tap and it is done.

  • Ask on every project. Even the small maintenance jobs. Volume builds credibility over time. More on this: How to Get More Google Reviews.
  • Answer the bad ones. A calm, professional reply matters even more in commercial, where the reviewer might be a decision-maker at a company that sends you repeat work.
