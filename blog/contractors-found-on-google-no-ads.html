The Contractor's Guide to Getting Found on Google Without Paying for Ads

You don't need a big ad budget to rank on Google. Here's how contractors show up in local search results using free strategies that compound over time.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 15, 20267 min read

Google Ads can eat $3,000 to $5,000 a month in competitive trades like roofing and HVAC. Some contractors can afford that. Many can't, especially in their first year or two.

The good news is that the organic results on Google, the listings below the ads, are free. So is the Map Pack. Getting into those spots takes time and effort instead of money, and once you're there, you stay there as long as you maintain it.

Here's what actually moves the needle.

Step 1: Google Business Profile first

This is the fastest path to local visibility and it costs nothing. Your GBP controls whether you show up in the Map Pack, which is the box of three businesses Google shows for local searches. The Map Pack gets 42 percent of all clicks on the first page.

What to do right now if you haven't already: claim your profile at business.google.com. Set your primary category to your specific trade (not "contractor" but "plumber" or "roofing contractor" or "HVAC contractor"). Add every service you offer as a separate service entry. Set your service area to the cities you actually serve. Upload at least 10 real photos of your work, your truck, and your team. Write a business description with your location and services mentioned naturally.

Then maintain it. Post an update every week or two. Add new photos after every job. Respond to every review. Google rewards active profiles with better placement.

Step 2: Build a website with real content

A one-page website with your phone number and a paragraph about your company isn't going to rank for anything useful. Google needs content to work with.

At minimum, build pages for your top five services, each targeting "[service] in [city]." Then add one or two location pages for your biggest markets. Then start a blog where you answer questions homeowners actually search for.

The blog is what scales. Each post is a new page that can rank for a new keyword. "How much does a roof replacement cost in [city]" or "what to do when your AC stops blowing cold air" bring in homeowners who are actively dealing with a problem you can solve.

You don't have to publish every day. Two posts a month adds up to 24 new ranking opportunities per year. After a year of that, your website is a lead machine.

Step 3: Reviews are a ranking factor

Google uses your review count and rating as a ranking signal for local search. A roofer with 180 reviews and a 4.9 rating will rank higher than a roofer with 15 reviews and the same rating, all else being equal.

This is the one ranking factor you can influence every single day. After every completed job, send a text with your Google review link. Keep it short and genuine: "Hey [name], thanks for letting us handle your [service]. If you've got a minute, a review would mean a lot: [link]."

Consistency beats volume. Two or three new reviews per week is better for your ranking than 30 all at once and then nothing for three months. Google likes a steady stream.

Step 4: Get listed in directories that matter

Not all directories are worth your time. The ones that move the needle for contractors: Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor (business page), Thumbtack (even if you don't buy leads, the listing helps SEO), your local Chamber of Commerce, and any trade-specific directories for your industry.

Each listing creates a "citation" with your business name, address, and phone number. Google cross-references these citations to verify your business is real and located where you say you are. Consistency matters. Your business name and address should be identical across every listing.

Skip the low-quality directories that email you saying "claim your free listing." If you've never heard of the directory, your customers haven't either, and the link isn't worth much to Google.

Step 5: Internal linking between your pages

This is the technical SEO move most contractor sites miss entirely. When you write a blog post about roof repair, link to your roof repair service page. When someone reads your service page about AC installation, link to a blog post about "how to choose the right AC unit for your house."

Internal links help Google understand the structure of your site and which pages are most important. They also keep visitors on your site longer, which Google tracks as a positive engagement signal.

A rule of thumb: every page on your site should link to at least two other pages. Every blog post should link to a relevant service page. This takes two minutes when you're publishing a new post and it compounds over time.

How long does this take to work?

Honest answer: three to six months before you see meaningful organic traffic. Google takes time to index and rank new content. Your GBP improvements will show results faster, usually within a few weeks.

After six months of consistent effort, most contractors see 20 to 40 percent of their leads coming from organic search. After a year, that number can be 50 percent or higher. That's money you're not spending on ads, and those leads belong to you forever as long as you maintain the site.

The contractors who start this today and stick with it will be the ones dominating their local search results a year from now. The ones who keep saying "I'll get to it next month" will keep paying for ads at next month's prices.
