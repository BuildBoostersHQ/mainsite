5 Texts Every Contractor Should Send After a Missed Call

Missed a call from a potential customer? These 5 text templates help contractors save the lead and book the job, even when you can't answer the phone.

By Madeleine JolieNovember 21, 20264 min read

You're on a ladder. You're under a sink. You're running a saw. The phone rings and you can't answer it.

That missed call is a potential job walking away. The homeowner hangs up and calls the next contractor on their list. By the time you check your phone at 5 PM, they've already booked someone else.

Missed-call text-back solves this. Within 60 seconds of that unanswered call, the homeowner gets a text from you. That text keeps them from calling your competitor and buys you time to call back when you're off the job.

Here are five texts that work.

1. The simple callback request

"Hey, this is [name] with [company]. Sorry I missed your call. I'm on a job right now but I'll call you back within the hour. What's the best time to reach you?"

This one works because it explains why you didn't answer (you're working, not ignoring them), commits to a callback, and starts a conversation. Most people reply with a time, and now you've got a confirmed lead in your text thread.

2. The service identifier

"Hey, thanks for calling [company]. I'm on site right now and can't pick up. What can I help you with? I'll get back to you as soon as I'm free."

This version works well if you offer multiple services because the homeowner will often reply with exactly what they need. "My AC is blowing warm air" gives you everything you need to call back prepared. You sound organized and you've already started diagnosing their problem.

3. The scheduling text

"Hi, this is [name] from [company]. Couldn't grab the phone. If you're looking for an estimate, I've got availability [tomorrow/this week]. Want me to get you on the schedule?"

This one pushes toward the booking immediately. For trades where the homeowner knows exactly what they need and just wants someone to come look at it, skipping the discovery conversation and going straight to scheduling can shorten the sales cycle.

4. The after-hours reply

"Hey, you've reached [company]. We're done for the day but I saw your call. I'll follow up first thing in the morning. If it's urgent, reply here and I'll see what I can do tonight."

After-hours calls are tricky because the homeowner might have an emergency and might be shopping around. This text tells them you saw their call, you'll follow up, and you're open to emergencies. It separates the "my toilet is flooding" calls from the "I want a quote on a new deck" calls so you can prioritize.

5. The warm personal touch

"Hey, this is [first name]. Missed your call while I was finishing up a [type of job] in [area]. Give me about 30 minutes and I'll ring you back. Or just shoot me a text and I'll reply between cuts."

This one works because it's specific and human. You're telling them what you were doing and where. It reads like a real person wrote it on the fly, not a marketing template. For a small operation where you're the owner and the technician, this personal approach builds trust before you've even spoken.

Automate it so you never have to think about it

The best version of missed-call text-back is the one that sends itself. You set it up once and every missed call gets an automatic text within 60 seconds. You never have to remember to do it. You never have to stop working to send a text.

The homeowner doesn't know it's automated. It reads like you personally sent it from your phone. And 90 percent of the time, they reply.

A contractor I work with tracked his missed-call text-back for three months. He missed 112 calls. 97 of them got the automated text. 68 of those people replied. He booked 31 jobs from calls he would have lost completely without the text.

31 jobs from calls he didn't even answer. That's the whole pitch for missed-call text-back right there.
